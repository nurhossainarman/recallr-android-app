## Vision Statement
Recallr is a focused study tool that allows students to create customizable flashcards to help them efficiently learn and review study material.


Recallr will encourage active recall and efficient study sessions by allowing users to create, organize, and review flashcard sets across a wide range of subjects. The system will store users’ decks and track their learning progress through review sessions. It will also maintain a history of which cards users have marked as learned and their mastery level for specific topics or subjects. In addition, the system will also provide tools for updating and reorganizing the flashcards as their learning needs evolve.


The application is primarily designed for high school and undergraduate students who rely heavily on memorization as part of their coursework or exam preparation. A student can access multiple study modes, including timed study sessions and review sessions with immediate feedback. Incorrectly answered cards will be re‑queued within the same session to ensure repeated exposure until mastery is demonstrated. The progress bar is intended to encourage consistency and boost their confidence.


Instructors and independent learners will also be able to use Recallr. Tutors may create decks for their courses and share them with students. Independent learners preparing for certifications or interviews may also use the system to test their proficiency level and review forgotten content.


Traditional methods of learning often promote passive rereading, which may seem useful but results in poor memory retention. Recallr addresses this problem by centering the study experience around active recall, repetition, and immediate feedback, helping students study more efficiently. The system is intended to reduce learners' cognitive load and tell them what they have mastered and what still needs work. Recallr should help learners build confidence, maintain consistency, and develop better study habits.


Recallr will be considered a success based on two key factors. First, a survey among high school and undergraduate students will be conducted to find out if the system facilitates effective learning and makes them feel more confident and prepared for their exams. Second, user engagement and performance, with at least 70% of active users finishing multiple study sessions per week and the majority of users showing reduced error rates over time.
